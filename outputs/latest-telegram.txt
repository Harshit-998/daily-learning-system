System Design: Consistency Models
Core idea: Consistency Models is the design tool used when choosing what users are allowed to observe during concurrent updates. In interviews, the important part is not the definition; it is deciding where this idea belongs in the request path and what trade-off it creates.
Trade-off: Consistency Models can improve one dimension such as latency, throughput, availability, or operability while increasing another dimension such as consistency complexity or debugging difficulty.

JS Interview: Promise scheduling, microtasks, and concurrency control
Question: What runs first: a resolved Promise callback, setTimeout(..., 0), or synchronous code?
Answer: Synchronous code runs first. Promise callbacks run next in the microtask queue after the current call stack finishes. setTimeout callbacks run later in the macrotask/timer phase.

DSA: Backtracking with Pruning
Notice: Backtracking with Pruning usually has a repeated decision that can be made locally once the right state is maintained.
Approach: For Backtracking with Pruning, ask: what information from the past is still relevant, what can be safely discarded, and what condition lets me commit to an answer?
Complexity: The optimized target is usually O(n), O(n log n), or O(states * transitions), depending on the pattern. Space is whatever state is required to preserve the invariant.
